Hunza Valley – Jewel of the Karakoram

A high mountain paradise of turquoise lakes, terraced orchards, ancient stone forts, and some of the most spectacular peaks on Earth.

Introduction

Welcome to Hunza

Tucked into the far north of Pakistan along the legendary Karakoram Highway, the Hunza Valley is famed for its breathtaking scenery, crystal-clear air, and the longevity and warmth of its people. Surrounded by giants such as Rakaposhi, Ultar Sar, and Lady Finger Peak, the valley is a dream destination for travellers, photographers, and mountaineers alike.

Spring brings a sea of pink and white blossom, while autumn sets the orchards ablaze in gold and crimson — making Hunza one of the most photographed regions in all of Pakistan.

Must-see

Top Attractions in Hunza

From a famous turquoise lake to forts that have watched over the valley for centuries.

Attabad Lake

Formed in 2010 after a landslide, Attabad Lake is now one of Hunza's most iconic sights — a ribbon of vivid turquoise water stretching over 20 kilometres between towering cliffs. Boating, jet-skiing, and lakeside cafés have turned this once-tragic site into a thriving tourist hub.

Baltit & Altit Forts

Perched above the village of Karimabad, the 700-year-old Baltit Fort and the even older Altit Fort once guarded the valley and housed its rulers, the Mirs of Hunza. Beautifully restored, they offer commanding views and a window into centuries of mountain history.

Passu Cones & Rakaposhi

The jagged "Passu Cones" are among the most distinctive peaks in the world, while Rakaposhi (7,788 m) rises dramatically above the valley floor. Viewpoints along the Karakoram Highway make these giants accessible to every traveller.
