Introduction

Welcome to Lahore

Lahore is the capital of Punjab and the second-largest city in Pakistan. Affectionately known as the "City of Gardens" for the many parks and green spaces laid out during the Mughal era, Lahore is celebrated for its rich heritage, vibrant bazaars, legendary cuisine, and warm hospitality. A local saying captures its spirit: one who has not seen Lahore has not truly lived.

The three landmarks below are the jewels of the old city and are unmissable for any visitor exploring the heart of Pakistan's Mughal legacy.

Must-see

Top Attractions in Lahore

Three UNESCO-worthy masterpieces of Mughal architecture, all within the historic walled city.

Badshahi Mosque

Commissioned by Emperor Aurangzeb in 1671 and completed in 1673, the Badshahi Mosque was the largest mosque in the world for over 300 years. Built from red sandstone with elegant marble inlay, its vast courtyard can hold tens of thousands of worshippers. The three grand marble domes and four towering minarets dominate the Lahore skyline and frame the adjacent Lahore Fort.

Lahore Fort (Shahi Qila)

The Lahore Fort, locally called the Shahi Qila, is a citadel rebuilt and expanded by successive emperors, reaching its peak under Akbar, Jahangir, and Shah Jahan. Inside its walls lie the dazzling Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors), the marble Naulakha Pavilion, and the monumental Alamgiri Gate. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981.

Shalimar Gardens

Laid out by Emperor Shah Jahan in 1641, the Shalimar Gardens are a stunning example of the Persian-inspired charbagh (four-garden) layout. Arranged across three descending terraces and adorned with more than 400 fountains, marble pavilions, and cascading water channels, the gardens were designed as an earthly paradise. Like the fort, they are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
